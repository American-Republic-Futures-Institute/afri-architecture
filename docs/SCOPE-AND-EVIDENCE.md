# Federal Institutional Baseline — Evidence and Coverage

**Date:** 2026-10-15. **Status:** research_baseline_unratified. **Evidence posture:** traced to public federal primary sources; not a legal opinion.

The reference structure is corroborated by the House of Representatives' branches overview, the White House's executive-branch description, the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts and the GPO's U.S. Government Manual (2025). Specific legislative and judicial support entities have additional institutional source records. Government agency missions are **summaries**, not direct quotations.

Coverage includes 3 branches; 15 executive departments; the President, Vice President and selected EOP components; selected executive independent establishments, boards and commissions; Congress, both chambers and primary legislative support agencies; main federal court categories and judiciary support. Agency existence does not establish that every claimed executive power is discretionary or that federal law displaces state jurisdiction.

**As-of caveat:** Organizations and legal authority change over time. The GPO government chart is a 2025 snapshot; cross-reference the live White House, House, U.S. Courts and USAGov directories for this 2026 baseline. Exact activation dates, agency reorganizations, transfer of functions, acting leaders and funding status are out of scope. In particular, the Education Department is retained among the 15 statutory departments; agency-level function transfers should be separately modeled when verified.

### Deliberate exclusions

- Constituent offices, congressional committees and caucuses, full congressional legislative operations.
- All DoD military departments/services, DHS component agencies, intelligence-community components, inspector general offices, thousands of bureaus and field locations.
- State, local, territorial and tribal governments and jurisdictional relationships.
- All Article I tribunals and administrative adjudication boards. The Court of Federal Claims is specially labeled as an Article I court administered within the federal judiciary; do not assume all Article I courts belong to the Article III judiciary.
- Formal enabling-statute traceability for each modern agency and any claim of policy impact, cost, outcome, or government reform endorsement.

### Interpretation precautions

Federal Reserve Board independence and U.S. Postal Service statutory status are not equivalent to Cabinet membership. The Defense/War alias is distinct from a statutory renaming. The counts 13 appeals courts, 94 district courts and 90 bankruptcy courts describe different court aggregates. The Supreme Court's appellate jurisdiction does not mean it takes every appeal. `exercises_oversight_of` refers to constitutionally and statutorily constrained mechanisms, not executive chain of command.

### Evidence standards for future revisions

Require dated primary source plus enabling statute for detailed authority claims; where sources conflict, record both and refer uncertain assertions to legal review. Preserve previous publication snapshots for reproducibility and archive superseded names with dates.
